# Choosing the Mass Desire

## **What is Mass Desire?**

Mass desire is the public spread of a private want - It’s when a desire is shared by masses of people. It can come in the form of hopes, dreams, fears…as long as it exists within millions of people. The mass desire must already exist. We, as marketers, cannot create it. So our goal is to not create desire, it’s to direct and channel it onto our product. For example, it’s not our job to create the desire of millions to lose weight. That will always exist. Our job is to *channel that desire onto a particular product.* 

## **Our Goal in Creating Our Message:**

- Identify the desires that already exist within the target consumer
- Identify the most powerful desire our product can fulfill
- Tell the story that links the pre-existing offer to our product

## **Why is it important to choose a mass desire?**

We can only take the hopes, dreams, fears and desires that already exist in the hearts of millions of people, and focus those already-existing desires onto our products. Choosing a mass desire is the most important step in creating a successful ad. If we don’t channel the most powerful desire, it’ll hamper the effectiveness of our messaging.

Mass desire is a foundational concept in marketing, **particularly relevant when you're crafting a creative concept strategy.** It revolves around the collective wants, needs, and aspirations of a target market. 

Understanding mass desire is crucial for developing effective strategies that resonate with your audience, ultimately influencing their buying behaviors or engagement levels. Here's how it ties into creative concept strategy:

## **Can we choose more than 1 mass desire?**

For each creative concept, only 1 can predominate. Attempting to give multiple desires attention will muddy the message.

However, it’s usually very acceptable to highlight a max 3 benefits in a standard UGC video. One being the mass desire & the main desire. 

## **What are the types of desires?**

People have deep, unchanging desires. Think hopes, dreams, fears, needs etc.

## **How do we choose the mass desire?**

Every mass desire has 3 vital dimensions. **The goal is to choose the mass desire that gives you the most power in all 3 dimensions.**

- Dimension #1: Urgency, intensity, degree of demand to be satisfied
- Dimension #2: staying power, degree of repetition, inability to become satisfied
- Dimension #3: Scope - the # of people who share this desire

Every product can appeal to 2, 3, or even 4 mass desires. But only one can predominate. Your choice among the alternate desires is the most important step you’ll take in creating your ad.

# **5 Levels Of Market Sophistication**

Definition owner for market sophistication. [Consumer awareness](#5-levels-of-consumer-awareness) below owns the separate awareness stages. Other methods link here.

<aside>
💡 In short, understanding market sophistication means that **your organization understands exactly how many similar products and/or services the marketplace you're operating in knows of already**.

</aside>

This principle describes how markets evolve over time and how advertising strategies should adapt to remain effective. 

Here are the five levels in detail:

## Level 1 - First to the Claim:

No one has made this promise to these buyers before. State the need or the claim directly, dramatise it, and prove it.

**How to Tell You're at This Stage:** Competitors do not make this promise, and buyers do not recognise it. (Whether buyers know they have the problem is a separate question: that is awareness, below.)

**Example**: The initial introduction of personal computers into the marketplace. Ads simply had to present the idea of a personal computer and explain the basic benefits.

**Example**: Buy Now: The world’s first and only [insert product or service here]

Ideally, you’re attempting to tell as many people as possible about your new product, and how it solves problems in a completely unseen way, so you can capture as much as the market as possible before competitors – and potentially regulators – step into the equation.

## Level 2 - The Claim Is Known and Still Works:

Others make the same promise, and it still sells. Make the claim bigger or more specific than theirs.

**How to Tell You're at This Stage:** Several competitors repeat the same promise, and buyers still respond to it.

Example: When Apple released the iPhone, they emphasized its unique features and superior usability compared to other smartphones on the market.

**Example: The [insert product/service here] that is twice as effective as the competition**

We move past the concept of having a one-of-a-kind product or service, and move into a more familiar arena of having a product or service with fierce competition.

## Level 3 - The Claims Are Exhausted:

Buyers have heard every version of the promise. A new, true mechanism — a new way the promise is achieved — makes it believable again.

**How to Tell You're at This Stage:** Bigger claims no longer lift response, and buyers say "they all say that".

Example: The modern smartphone market is saturated with many companies producing similar devices. Brands like Samsung introduce new and improved features to stand out.

**Example:** the time-saving measures of a piece of accounting software “saving businesses [x amount of time],” or a new washing machine that can reduce water use while maintaining a quality clean.

In the third level of market sophistication, as you can probably guess, those in the market are increasingly aware of the claims made for your products and services, but they don’t yet understand exactly how the whole thing works. 

<aside>
💡 **This can be achieved not by explicitly selling your product, but marketing the *how* of your product or service.**

</aside>

### Level 4 - The Mechanisms Are Known:

Competitors already sell on mechanisms. Enlarge or improve the known mechanism: easier, quicker, surer, or solving more of the problem.

**How to Tell You're at This Stage:** Competitor ads explain how their products work, and new mechanisms appear often. (If the main barrier is distrust rather than familiar mechanisms, use the proof and de-risking route in [the mature-market response chooser](mature-market-response-chooser.md).)

**Example:** once "dual-action" formulas are common in a category, a brand shows how its version needs half the steps, or reaches the part of the problem the older mechanism missed.

You need to ensure that the claims you’re making to customers are in fact truthful, otherwise you risk abusing their trust, losing business and even facing penalties from regulators.

### **Level 5: Claims No Longer Persuade:**

Buyers disbelieve the whole category. They respond to seeing themselves in the ad more than to any promise or mechanism.

**How to Tell You're at This Stage:** Distrust of the category is widespread, and identity and community signals outperform claims.

- Example: Many luxury car brands appeal to the customer's self-image or aspirations, such as Mercedes-Benz using the slogan "The Best or Nothing" to evoke feelings of prestige and quality beyond the specific features of their cars.

## Now How Does This Impact Our Marketing Strategy?

1. **First to the claim:** state it plainly and prove it.
2. **Claim known, still working:** enlarge or sharpen the claim.
3. **Claims exhausted:** introduce a new, true mechanism.
4. **Mechanisms known:** improve on the known mechanism.
5. **Claims no longer persuade:** lead with identification, backed by proof.

Sophistication describes how many claims buyers have heard. It is separate from awareness, which describes what a buyer knows about the problem, solutions and product. One market can contain groups at different levels, and the level is a working call from evidence, not a measurement.


# 5 Levels of Consumer Awareness

Definition owner for the five awareness stages. [Growthcraft 2.1.2](growthcraft.md#212-stage-of-awareness-lens-soa) owns its application, not a second stage list. Awareness concerns what the buyer knows; [market sophistication](#5-levels-of-market-sophistication) concerns familiarity with competing claims.

It’s important to meet your customers where they already are. Consider the five level of awareness to be a mini funnel. 

If you deliver the improper message or ad content about your solution to a consumer who isn’t even aware they have n problem, they may be scared off. 

You cannot expect your top of funnel audience to consume marketing material designed for those at the bottom of the funnel in the same way - it will not be appropriate for them.

With the 5 levels of customer awareness, you have the opportunity to do that because you know exactly how to market to them

# Level 1: Unaware

The first level of awareness is for customers who are completely unaware that they even experience said problem. This level is the beginning point for your sales funnel. At this level, customers are not ready to make a purchase because, in their mind, they don’t have a problem and wouldn’t benefit from your solution.

- They don’t know that a better way exists
- Content to live with the pain point they feel forever
- No knowledge of your product or service exists in their mind.

**Example:** You are constantly wasting food and spending money on take-out. You don’t realize that this is a problem and will continue to live your life spending the extra money to keep you and your family fed.

**How to Market:** Begin with a situation or mindset the audience recognises, then make the relevance clear. A problem, product, desire or solution can appear when the execution makes it understandable; do not assume prior category knowledge.

# Level 2: **Problem Aware**

In this phase, customers who are aware that they have a problem but may not completely understand it or what solutions are out there. The main frustration for a problem-aware customer is that they are experiencing a problem that they are aware of but can’t figure out how to fix it.

- Aware that they’re experiencing a problem, they feel the pain.
- They are not familiar with your business or others like yours.
- Can’t figure out how to fix it.

**Example:** You realize that you are wasting food and money and want to change that, but don’t know that a better way exists. You are now constantly living with this pain point.

**How to Market:** Campaigns to highlight that the customer doesn’t have to experience the problem they are facing. Stress that a solution exists through articles, social media posts, and paid advertising.

# Level 3: **Solution Aware**

Fully aware of the problem AND aware that a potential solution exists. They know the results they want and know that solutions like yours exist; however, they have not heard of your company.

- Aware of the pain of their problem
- Aware solutions exists
- Know the end results they’re looking for
- Unaware of YOUR solution / brand

**Example:** You are finally ready to find a solution for the problem you are facing. You begin to read informational pieces on food waste and learn that digital and manual solutions exist that could solve the problem.

**How to Market:** Empathize with the customer and help them begin to look for a solution. When they are in that mindset then you can present them with articles about your solution and why your business exists.

# Level 4: Product / Brand Aware

The customer has an understanding of the problem they face, they know solutions exist, and they are aware that your business offers a solution. They are looking for solutions and weighing the pros and cons. The only reservation they have is if your solution is best for them compared to others like yours.

- Aware of their problem, pain, potential solutions, and YOUR solution.
- The “researcher” phase
- Looking for solutions and weighing the pros and cons.

**Example:** You have done the research and read articles on various meal planning solutions. You have found three different meal planning apps but aren’t sure which one will work best.

**How to Market:** The number one thing to do is to present your solution as better than the others. Through email campaigns, paid advertising, and articles, use strong copy to describe the features of your solution. Use good reviews in advertisements that show your solution as number one on the market.

# Level 5: Most Aware

Most-aware buyers know the product and its relevant benefits, want it, and are ready to consider the specific offer or next buying step. They may be first-time buyers or existing customers. Awareness describes their knowledge and readiness; it does not establish loyalty, advocacy, future use or price sensitivity.

- Know what the product offers and have a reason to want it
- Need the relevant offer details or a clear path to act
- Assess prior relationship, loyalty and price sensitivity separately from buyer evidence

**How to Market:** Make the relevant offer and next action clear. Use price, availability, convenience or reassurance according to the buyer's remaining need. A discount can fit a price barrier; it is not required by awareness or loyalty. Review requests belong to the post-purchase relationship, not the definition of this stage.


**Example:** A first-time buyer knows which product they want and checks price, availability and delivery before buying. A past purchase alone does not establish readiness for the current offer.

## A practitioner's view: what the ad leads with at each level

This is one practitioner's shorthand, rebuilt here in our own words. It sits beside the stage definitions above and does not replace them. Its claim about how many marketers work at the last level, and its picture of the most-aware buyer, have not been checked; Level 5 above already says awareness does not establish price sensitivity.

1. **Unaware.** This is the largest audience. Lead with the viewer's own mindset or situation, in their voice, and name no problem, product, desire or solution. The shape is a first-person admission of how a day feels.
2. **Problem aware.** These people are feeling a problem right now. Lead with the problem itself. The shape is a blunt first-person statement of the problem with a plea for what to do about it.
3. **Solution aware.** Lead with the desire: the result people want but have not yet connected to this product. The shape is a promise of the easiest route to that result for a beginner.
4. **Product aware.** They know the brand and what it sells but are not sold. Prove it beats the competitors and build trust. The shape is a claim to be the only option they will ever need.
5. **Most aware.** The view says nine in ten marketers work here, and calls it the least skilled level, deserving the lowest pay: the ad simply names a discount to people who already know, like and trust the product and are price sensitive. The shape is the product name with a percentage off.
