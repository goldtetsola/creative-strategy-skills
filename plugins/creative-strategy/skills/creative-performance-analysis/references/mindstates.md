# Mindstates: motivation, framing and choice

Use mindstates to understand what a customer wants, why it matters, how they approach the decision, and which cues help them choose. Start with the goal, identify the motivation and the cautious or optimistic approach, then use the relevant profile to shape copy, visuals and the customer experience. The catalogue helps check the role of a specific decision shortcut.

A mindstate is the combination of two things: how your target approaches the decision (optimistic or cautious) and the core motivation driving their behaviour. It is activated in a temporary moment of high emotional arousal, called a hot state. The aim is to influence the customer at the moment of decision by messaging to this non-conscious mindstate. Use the profiles as an overlay to develop greater empathy with the mindstate influencing their attitudes, beliefs and behaviour.

## The four-part model

| Layer | Question | What changes in the work |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | What end state is the person trying to reach? | The outcome the offer makes relevant. |
| Motivation | What feeling makes that outcome matter? | The emotional reason to pursue it. |
| Regulatory approach | Do they want more good, or less bad? | The framing of the same capability. |
| Heuristic | What shortcut could simplify this choice? | The cue, comparison or next-step treatment. |

A goal gives a customer's decisions and behaviour their target: it is the distance between where they are now and the end state they want. Goals come in two types, functional and aspirational (emotional). The aspirational, higher-order kind guides our decisions and gives direction to daily life. Because every action heads towards some goal, known or not, your job is to understand those goals and subconsciously activate them by showing how your solution helps customers reach their aspirations.

### Research the goal

Ask what customers ask for; what matters most to them when using the product or service; why those things matter; what the purpose of buying the brand or category is; what buying it says about them; the top things they would tell others about the brand; and what their overall aspirational goal is and how reaching it would make them feel.

### The nine motivations

If a goal is the destination, motivation is the emotional fuel that moves people towards it. Every action has a motivation underneath it, and psychologically there are nine universal, distinct ones, each creating specific desires that drive all our actions. They work at a deep emotional level, so understanding a customer's true motivation helps shift them from intending to act into acting. Prime the need by linking your product or experience to that motivation.

To find it, ask which one of the nine best explains why customers really want to reach their goals, so that they can feel what? Then ask whether a secondary motivation could also be at play, and which one.

| Motivation | Desired feeling | Profile detail |
|---|---|---|
| Achievement | Successful, victorious and closure by overcoming obstacles. | Closure by beating others; being the best. |
| Autonomy | Free, independent and distinct from others. | Self-determination; free to act on their unique preferences. |
| Belonging | Aligned, accepted and connected with others. | Increased acceptance and affiliation with others. |
| Competence | Capable by being qualified, prepared and skilled in an activity. | Surpassing their own personal standards. |
| Empowerment | In control, authorised and equipped to act. | Better equipped with the power needed; less variability and lack of control. |
| Engagement | Captivated and fully absorbed in activities. | Excited, and also tranquil, happy moments. |
| Esteem | Approval, respect and admiration from others. | Feeling unique and praised. |
| Nurturance | Appreciated, loved and taken care of by others. | Also providing care and support, to others or themselves; being able to right a wrong. |
| Security | Secure, safe and protected from threats. | Assurance that they need not worry, because this is the safest decision or action. |

### Regulatory approaches

Once you know the motivation, the next job is to lower any cognitive resistance customers feel to your marketing or to taking the next step. Matching their approach does this. Either approach can frame your brand as the best way to reach the goal, so find the true dominant approach among your customers and frame your features to match: as helping them get more good, or avoid more bad.

**Optimistic / promotion:** people seek brands that help them maximise their chances of reaching their aspirational goal. Frame a feature around the good it adds.

**Cautious / prevention:** people seek brands that help them minimise their chances of risk or failure in reaching that goal. Frame a feature around the bad it helps avoid.

Security can be optimistic, and engagement can be cautious. To find the dominant approach, ask: when making decisions in your category, do your customers want "more good" and feel more optimistic, or do they want "less bad" and feel more cautious?

### Heuristics at a decision point

Heuristics are the fourth stage. Messaging that has activated the goal, primed the motivation and matched its benefits to the approach has likely triggered a hot state, the moment of psychological influence called a trigger point. Some people still need a small, additional nudge to buy, and a mental shortcut in the messaging provides it. We all use these triggers to make decisions faster and easier, because they eliminate the need for a cost–benefit analysis on every decision. Put one or two key heuristics near a call to action or purchase button and you can subconsciously trigger a sale or other behaviour.

Select only heuristics that help explain or design the treatment. There is no minimum count. Name the observed cue or intended role, and check any attribution against the trigger's profile or catalogue entry. A named heuristic does not establish that it caused a sale.

## Apply an overlay, then learn

Marketing first has to get past a filter. Research shows that the subconscious keeps more than 85% of all marketing from ever being consciously considered, and whatever does break through works less well because people have become psychologically resistant to all forms of marketing. Mindstates are the way past both. In a hot state people are more susceptible to influence, because they rely more on System 1: non-conscious, emotional processing in purchase decisions and behaviour. Strategy and marketing built around these subconscious mindstate blueprints can pass through the filter and engage customers psychologically, and that engagement is what raises their desire to buy from you.

Restaging a brand around mindstates takes three steps:

1. **Identify the dominant mindstates.** Each one has specific psychological characteristics that subconsciously drive people to act. Find the top two or three driving decisions in your category.
2. **Audit strategy and marketing through their lens.** Run a systematic audit of core customer touchpoints to see how well each aligns. At every point, ask what would better engage the customer's subconscious drives, desires, values, beliefs, behaviours and preferences.
3. **Activate them consistently.** Wherever your engagement and their mindstate part company, brainstorm ways to align them. The answer is often a simple tweak to how you interact with customers, so first use the profile as an overlay on what you already do and see how it works. Once results turn positive, stay consistent with those actions to build strong associations between your business and these subconscious drivers.

Focus on visuals first, then copy, when evoking feelings. The profiles apply to product development as well as messaging.

## OCEAN and archetype overlays

OCEAN, also known as the Big 5, is a way to understand personality through five traits: Openness, how curious and creative someone is; Conscientiousness, how organised and responsible they are; Extraversion, how outgoing they are; Agreeableness, how kind and cooperative they are; and Neuroticism, how emotionally stable they are. Brand marketers can use these traits to create advertisements and products that appeal to different types of people, and to connect better with their target audience. Use each profile's Big Five pattern to connect with customers and write to fit their natural style.

An archetype communicates your brand's personality and values consistently, which helps create a strong emotional connection with customers and differentiates the brand effectively. Use HERO for Achievement; EXPLORER for Autonomy; EVERYMAN for Belonging; SAGE for Competence; RULER for Empowerment and Esteem; JESTER for Engagement; CAREGIVER for Nurturance; and INNOCENT for Security. Both approaches of a motivation share its archetype. RULER means leadership, order and control for Empowerment, and exclusivity, excellence and distinction for Esteem. JESTER brings humour, spontaneity, joy and entertainment.

## Achievement: completion, victory and earned progress

### Cautious Achievement

**Decision logic, goal and frame.** This person needs success, accomplishment and closure, and gets them by beating others, being the best and overcoming obstacles. In the cautious state that drive becomes persistent and relentless about avoiding mistakes: they want tools that limit risk and clear any barrier to the goal. They believe expertise and tried-and-true processes are the best way to a predictable result, so brands with an honest, transparent record of success are significantly advantaged. They work slowly and deliberately, look for positive feedback that they are on track, and want concrete reasons and examples of how the solution brings success. The process of winning is valued as much as the success itself, and successful closure with a reward is highly desired. The goal to activate is reaching their goals by maximising their potential through increased power, dedication and energy. Show how the brand helps them reach it and feel victorious. Frame the message around how the brand minimises their chances of losing.

**OCEAN profile:** Conscientiousness is high: they set and keep high standards for themselves. Neuroticism is low: they stay composed and work methodically to avoid mistakes. Openness and agreeableness are moderate: they take to new ideas within their chosen areas of competence, and put personal competence above harmony with others. They are potentially more introverted, focused on internal growth. They value predictability, reliability and a track record of success, so present a dependable, trustworthy solution that reduces risk and ensures a successful outcome, using proven processes, transparency and concrete examples of success.

**Local triggers:**
1. Peak End Rule → [H079](#h061-to-h080): The tendency to judge an entire experience largely based on how they felt at its peak (i.e., its most intense point) and at its end, rather than the entire experience in aggregate. This is very useful when showcasing the point of overcoming obstacles.
2. Egocentric Bias → [X01](#x01-egocentric-bias): The tendency to believe that positive results are the result of your individual actions vs. chance or other outside influence.
3. Sunk Cost Fallacy → [H108](#h101-to-h109): The tendency to justify increased investment of money, time, lives, etc. in a decision, based on the cumulative prior investment. In this mindstate people are more likely to value the "sunk costs" associated with trying to achieve their goal.
4. Self-Justification → [X02](#x02-self-justification): The tendency to justify a behavior and deny any negative feedback associated with the behavior to reach a goal.

**Emotional direction:** less inferior, unmotivated, complacent, unsuccessful, overcome, failed and ineffective; more tenacious, worthy, determined, successful and victorious, with more closure and accomplishment. Evoke these authentically.

**Brand and archetype:** HERO through determination, energy, persistence and the pursuit of success against all odds, with strength, resilience and a deep sense of purpose. Present the brand as an unstoppable force, restless and relentless in pursuing greatness. Refuse mediocrity and challenge the customer to do the same. Act as visionaries with a focused mission to help them reach their wildest dreams, and as a steadfast partner who reduces risk and obstacles with expertise and proven processes. Treat the journey as worth as much as the final triumph. Play a supporting role, honoured and humbled to witness their triumphs and inspired by their commitment. For more on the archetype, see *The Hero and the Outlaw*.

**Creative and copy:** highlight the pain of disappointment from failing, and show how the solution helps them avoid failure. Name the specific features that limit barriers to success, and the process and structure behind the product's design that help overcome them. Show people working together for greater advancement or success. Let joy and pride peak at the moment a formidable barrier is avoided. Keep the body contained, with reserved gestures and posture and a slower tempo. Have the model look straight into the camera, because eye contact draws viewers into the final moment of success. Stay close and in focus on the product and the moment a barrier to achievement falls, against a simple, clean background.

Pictures should focus on accomplishment, determination and success. Show people pursuing goals through planned steps, checking likely obstacles and persisting to completion. Show the process of winning as well as the final moment: hard work, obstacles overcome and small victories celebrated along the way. Show the checks, practice or proven process that helps people avoid obstacles. A new tool can fit when its evidence supports more reliable progress; novelty alone is not the reason to choose it.

Write in a professional, confident and results-oriented voice that stresses the brand's expertise and record of reaching goals and removing obstacles, and names the specific ways it helps. Be enthusiastic and motivating: show that success is within reach through hard work, persistence and the brand's support. Apply [shared practice](#achievement-experience--both-approaches).

**What the examples teach:** treat these as starting points for unique selling propositions. Open on an everyday goal, short- or long-term, such as weight to lose or a reward at work, and the pitfall the person is steering round on the way to the sweet feeling of success. Talk about the next step rather than the finish: slot in the customer's goal, the obstacle caught early, the process behind the progress and a milestone they can watch. Two clipped statements side by side suit this voice. Taglines treat success as a series of goals, as a journey with the brand as guide, or as potential the brand opens up. Every capability or result claimed needs product evidence.

### Optimistic Achievement

**Decision logic, goal and frame.** The motive is the same: success, accomplishment and closure through beating others, being the best and overcoming obstacles. Here it drives relentless action to reach the goal as quickly as possible, even if that adds risk. They want to take chances and seize untapped opportunities. They look for openings, work quickly, consider many alternatives and seek positive feedback that they are on track, and they prefer novel, innovative solutions that significantly increase their chance of success. Winning is just as important as the final moment of success, and closure with a reward is also highly desired. They value the accomplishment that comes from putting commitment and energy into overcoming obstacles. They believe they are worthy and personally responsible for their results, rather than circumstance or luck, so brands and products are essential but ancillary. The goal to activate is reaching their goals by maximising their potential through increased power, dedication and energy. Show how the brand helps them reach it and feel victorious. Frame the message around how the brand maximises their potential to win.

**OCEAN profile:** Openness is moderate to high: they welcome risk and novel approaches. Agreeableness is moderate, and open to innovative approaches. Conscientiousness is moderate: accomplishment comes first, with a willingness to take calculated risks. Neuroticism is moderate, motivated by the desire for successful outcomes. They are neutral on extroversion, with the emphasis on personal responsibility. They value rapid achievement, so show innovative, unconventional solutions that significantly increase the chance of quick success. Focus on seizing opportunities, personal agency and concrete examples of successful closure with rewards.

**Local triggers:**
1. Peak End Rule → [H079](#h061-to-h080): The tendency to judge an entire experience largely based on how they felt at its peak (i.e., its most intense point) and at its end, rather than the entire experience in aggregate. This is very useful when showcasing the point of overcoming obstacles.
2. Egocentric Bias → [X01](#x01-egocentric-bias): The tendency to believe that positive results are the result of your individual actions vs. chance or other outside influence.
3. Sunk Cost Fallacy → [H108](#h101-to-h109): The tendency to justify increased investment of money, time, lives, etc. in a decision, based on the cumulative prior investment. In this mindstate people are more likely to value the "sunk costs" associated with trying to achieve their goal.
4. Self-Justification → [X02](#x02-self-justification): The tendency to justify a behavior and deny any negative feedback associated with the behavior to reach a goal.

**Emotional direction:** more tenacious, motivated, determined, successful, victorious and accomplished, with more closure; less inferior, unmotivated, complacent, unsuccessful, overcome, failed and ineffective. Evoke these authentically.

**Brand and archetype:** HERO through determination, energy, persistence and the pursuit of success against all odds, in a supporting role to the customer's own commitment. Help customers explore useful alternatives and act on opportunities, including calculated risks when the potential gain justifies them. Show how the product supports their effort and momentum; evidence still matters when the approach is new. Never settle for less, and tell the customer not to either. Position the brand as the visionary that uses innovation to turn calculated risks and relentless persistence into a smashing success for them, crafting ingenious, imaginative solutions that greatly improve their chance of true success. Be highly motivated and self-assured, always exploring novel and creative solutions. Take full ownership of success and never shy away from alternative paths to help them get there. For more on the archetype, see *The Hero and the Outlaw*.

**Creative and copy:** highlight the pleasure of success when winning with the solution. Say why it makes people successful, and claim the best innovations. Emphasise change, taking chances, innovation and seizing opportunity to win. Show the customer's independence and how the brand contributes to their success. Show determined, confident expressions, above all when a barrier to success is being overcome. Let the body move freely, with animated gestures, a faster cadence, open arms and posture, a straight back and chin up. Turn the model away from the camera so the viewer becomes a witness to the moment of success. Use broader views of the product and that moment against abstract, slightly unfocused backgrounds.

Pictures should focus on accomplishment, determination and success. Show individuals or groups actively pursuing goals, persisting and taking calculated risks. Show the process of winning, including hard work, obstacles overcome and small victories along the way. Show innovation and creativity, demonstrating the brand's preference for novel approaches to problem-solving that increase the chance of success.

Write optimistically, confidently and inspiringly about the benefits of persistence, calculated risks and innovative approaches. Inspire people to act today, even if only on a small step, so they make progress by partnering with the brand. Be enthusiastic and motivating: show that success is within reach through hard work, persistence and the brand's support. Apply [shared practice](#achievement-experience--both-approaches).

**What the examples teach:** treat these as starting points for unique selling propositions. Open on an everyday goal and the steps the person is already taking toward it, with hard work and the sweet feeling of success in view. Command action with verbs of taking charge and seizing, and present the brand as the top ally in winning. Name the competition to get ahead of and a fast-changing world. State maxims that tie success to determination, hard work and pushing limits, and invite a first step. Taglines are the same family as the cautious set: potential opened up, success goal by goal, a journey with a guide, dreams made real.

### Achievement experience — both approaches

Do not overstep. Never suggest that the brand will ultimately lead to their success: these people believe they will succeed with or without it. Position it as a critical tool they can use for ultimate success, and nothing more. The customer is the hero; the brand is the equipment they carry.

Let them do the winning. Help them set firm, distinct goals compared with others, and increase successful levelling or rewards while they use the brand. Make them aware of the important milestones they reach. Let them apply their extraordinary abilities and overcome an otherwise difficult obstacle through sheer will and personal ability.

In these moments they see themselves as powerful or influential individuals. If an ad has people in it, set the lead model apart from the others with traits that signal success psychologically: eye colour, a stronger or more muscular build, greater height, sharper facial features, or unique or special clothes. Empathy tends to run lower, because people and circumstances more often look like barriers on the way to success. Comparison marketing works here: your competitive strength persuades more in these mindstates than in others.

## Autonomy: freedom, individuality and personal choice

### Cautious Autonomy

**Decision logic, goal and frame.** The motive is independence, sufficiency and self-determination: being free to act on unique preferences so they can reach the goal they want. In today's world, where choices can feel overly restrictive, the cautious version of this is feeling distinct while avoiding anything that restricts or controls them. They want tools that get them what they want, the way they want it, in the time and place they want it. Being unbounded to choose their own path is highly desired, and uniqueness and freedom come from self-determination and from affirming their ability to act and customise. They believe in building or finding distinct, customisable experiences for their seemingly unique needs, and that avoiding controlled experiences is vital to success. They work slowly and deliberately to avoid anything that could limit their freedom, and prefer tried and true solutions with concrete reasons and examples of how the solution lets them find, build or customise the perfect fit. Give concrete, specific examples of autonomy rather than abstract, general ones. The goal to activate is reaching their goals by avoiding what may lessen their freedom or personalisation. Show how the brand gives them more freedom. Frame the message around how the brand minimises the risk to their freedom or individuality.

**OCEAN profile:** Openness is moderate to high: they take to unique, customised solutions. Agreeableness is moderate: they accept tried and true solutions that can be customised. Conscientiousness is moderate: they value individuality and freedom and are not highly structured. Neuroticism is low, motivated by the desire for freedom and autonomy. They are neutral on extroversion, with the emphasis on personal freedom. Show how the offer allows personalised experiences and the freedom to choose in line with distinct preferences, with concrete examples of how it enables self-determination and unique solutions.

**Local triggers:**
1. Egocentric Bias → [X01](#x01-egocentric-bias): The tendency to believe that positive results are the result of individual actions vs. chance or other outside influences.
2. Projection Bias → [H085](#h081-to-h100): The tendency to falsely project current preferences onto future products and events.
3. Endowment Effect → [H032](#h021-to-h040): The tendency to ascribe more value to things merely because they are owned.
4. IKEA Effect → [H046](#h041-to-h060): The tendency to place a disproportionately high value on products that one has built or created.

**Emotional direction:** less dependent, restricted, predictable, conforming, limited, controlled and forgettable; more independent, authentic, surprising, distinct, unique, different and free. Evoke these authentically.

**Brand and archetype:** EXPLORER—independence, self-determination and individuality, in the spirit of adventure and discovery. Understand that customers value freedom of choice and expressing their unique preferences, and provide tools and solutions that get them what they want, the way they want it, when and where they want it. Be the individuality-focused brand that builds or finds distinct, customisable experiences for unique needs and actively avoids controlled or limiting ones, working slowly and deliberately. Be the brand of choice for those unbounded in their pursuit of personalisation, who crave the freedom to create their own path. For more on the archetype, see *The Hero and the Outlaw*.

**Creative and copy:** highlight the pain of being restricted and how the solution limits restrictions, and claim the fewest limitations. Emphasise stability, accuracy and control in limiting constraints. Show people working together to limit constraints, and how the solution adds to the person's freedom to choose and customise. Capture eagerness and liberation in moments of individual choice. Hold the body in, with reserved gestures, a slower cadence and closed arms and posture. Have the model look into the camera, because eye contact draws viewers into the moment of self-determination. Stay in focus and close on the product and the moment a possible limit on choice is overcome, against a simple, clean background.

Pictures should focus on freedom, individuality and personalisation. Show customers unbounded in their pursuit of personalisation, diverse people with unique styles and preferences to convey inclusivity and personal expression, and innovative tools and products that show the brand's customisable solutions.

Write positively, encouragingly and enthusiastically, with excitement about the possibilities of freedom of choice and personalisation. Inspire people to find their unique voice and personality by partnering with the brand to customise their experience, to embrace their individuality and to take control of their unique journey. Apply [shared practice](#autonomy-experience--both-approaches).

**What the examples teach:** treat these as starting points for unique selling propositions. Open on a world where choices feel overly restrictive and on the individuality people have built through life and want to keep. Set restriction against possibility in short, opposed beats. Use imperatives of choosing, creating and breaking free, with the customer as author of their own path or destiny. Let the customer feel rebellious against the status quo and flattered as unlike anyone else, even extraordinary. Promise a fit with their unique needs through customisable, personalised solutions.

### Optimistic Autonomy

**Decision logic, goal and frame.** The motive is to be unique, independent and self-determining, free to act on unique preferences so they can reach their goals. In the optimistic state they feel distinct by expressing those preferences, and look for customisation in pursuing their goals, even if that adds risk. They want tools that get them what they want, the way they want it, in the time and place they want it. Being unbounded to choose their own path is highly desired, and they value the uniqueness and freedom that come from the authority to personalise with unbounded choice. They look for chances to gain more freedom to customise, work quickly, consider many alternatives and stay open to new possibilities, and they prefer novel, innovative solutions that let them find or build the perfect fit. The goal to activate is reaching their goals by maximising what may increase their uniqueness and freedom. Show how the brand helps customers stand out from the crowd or have more freedom. Frame the message around how the brand maximises their potential for freedom.

**OCEAN profile:** Openness is high: they take to unique, customised solutions even when these involve risk. Conscientiousness is moderate to high: they value individuality and freedom, with a flexible approach. Agreeableness is moderate, and open to innovation and customisation. Neuroticism is low, motivated by the desire for freedom and autonomy in customisation. They are neutral on extroversion, with the emphasis on personal freedom. Show innovative, customisable solutions that empower them to choose in line with distinct preferences, with concrete examples of personalisation and unbounded choice.

**Local triggers:**
1. Egocentric Bias → [X01](#x01-egocentric-bias): The tendency to believe that positive results are the result of individual actions vs. chance or other outside influences.
2. Projection Bias → [H085](#h081-to-h100): The tendency to falsely project current preferences onto future products and events.

4. IKEA Effect → [H046](#h041-to-h060): The tendency to place a disproportionately high value on products that one has built or created.

**Emotional direction:** the fourteen autonomy feelings in [Cautious Autonomy](#cautious-autonomy) also apply here, with expansion of freedom rather than prevention of its loss as the frame. Evoke these authentically.

**Brand and archetype:** EXPLORER through independence, self-determination and individuality, in the spirit of adventure and discovery. Let a desire for uniqueness and freedom drive the brand. Offer tools and experiences with customisation, even if it means taking risks, and value the authority to personalise on one's own terms. Take pride in novel, innovative solutions that let customers build and find the perfect fit. Be the ultimate destination for those who value their freedom and individuality, and the brand of choice for those who are unbounded in their pursuit of personalisation and crave the freedom to create their own path. For more on the archetype, see *The Hero and the Outlaw*.

**Creative and copy:** highlight the pleasure of self-determination with the solution. Say why it provides freedom, and claim the most options. Emphasise change, taking chances, innovation and seizing opportunity through individuality. Show the customer's independence and how the solution contributes to their free will. Show inquisitive, liberated expressions, above all when someone is being recognised as unique or free. Let the body move freely, with animated gestures, a faster cadence, open arms and posture, a straight back and chin up. Turn the model away from the camera so the audience witnesses the moment of greater personalisation. Use broader views of the product and the moment of choice against abstract, slightly unfocused backgrounds.

Inspire people to find their unique voice and personality by partnering with the brand to customise their experience, to embrace their individuality and to take control of their unique journey. Apply [shared practice](#autonomy-experience--both-approaches).

**What the examples teach:** treat these as starting points for unique selling propositions. Open on the freedom to live on one's own terms and to express personal style in what they buy. Dare them: short, bold imperatives stacked in threes, with risk presented as something to take on. Reject solutions made for everyone. Cast the customer as the designer of their own life and shrink the brand to the tools they use. Lean on repeated "your". Pair personalisation with innovation, and talk of potential being released.

### Autonomy experience — both approaches

Let customers build their experiences on their terms and feel unique while using the brand, though not necessarily by making them become special. Give them great self-direction and latitude so they own the process. Offer a variety of perspectives and examples that develop their individual tastes and preferences, and let them find and map unique connections between seemingly dissimilar products, services and ideas. Help them feel positive about their freedom through meaningful feedback, customised choices and options.

Cautious autonomy asks which limitations are avoided; optimistic autonomy asks what new expression becomes possible.

## Belonging: acceptance and a dependable connection

### Cautious Belonging

**Decision logic, goal and frame.** The motive is to feel aligned, accepted and connected with others, in a world where lasting relationships are increasingly hard to build. The desire is often so powerful that people will go to great lengths to avoid disharmony. In the cautious state they avoid mistakes that could get them excluded, and validate their sense of social acceptance by avoiding repudiation by a larger group. They want tools that establish and maintain relationships by removing barriers to common ground or camaraderie. They value the community that comes from not being disliked or rejected, or from shared interests that can be counted on when working together, and they believe deeper, more meaningful connections validate their social acceptability. ### Optimistic Security

**Decision logic, goal and frame.** In a chaotic world, almost everyone wants to feel more secure, and people work every day to protect those they love and what they own from loss or misuse. Relief at being free from worry is the root of this mindstate. People seek the assurance that they will not have to worry about a decision or action because it is the safest one to make. Here they look to immediately increase their ability to protect themselves from loss or pain and to deal with threats while pursuing a goal, maximising their chance of personal or psychological security. Strong experience and expertise in adding protection are highly desired, as is knowing every precaution has been taken. Specific plans and detailed, increased monitoring for risk are key to enjoying the experience, and more safeguards bring inner peace and trust. They believe predictable outcomes through expertise and tried-and-true processes are the best route to their goals, so brands established as honest and transparent, with a track record of success, are significantly advantaged. They look for opportunity, act quickly, consider alternatives and seek reassurance that their decisions are good and consistent. They prefer "second generation" innovation that has been tested to provide more predictable, safe outcomes. Frame the brand as the way to maximise their potential to increase safety.

**OCEAN profile:** O low–moderate, focused on security and risk reduction; C high, valuing security, meticulous planning and risk reduction; E neutral, emphasising predictable outcomes; A moderate, preferring established, proven solutions; N moderate, motivated by security and peace of mind. These levels match Cautious Security. Show how the product enhances security, increases protection and provides peace of mind, with concrete examples of predictable outcomes and added safeguards, while maintaining transparency and trustworthiness.

**Local triggers:**
1. Hyperbolic Discounting → [H045](#h041-to-h060): The tendency to place higher value on rewards that are immediate vs. longer-term.
2. Loss Aversion → [H063](#h061-to-h080): preference for avoiding a loss over an equivalent gain. If a buyer seeking more protection also wants to avoid a specific loss, compare that supported treatment with an equivalent gain in protection. Keep the wider goal of increasing safety; do not infer risk appetite or loss aversion from the profile label alone.
3. Conformity Effect → [X03](#x03-conformity-effect): The tendency to place higher value on actions that others "like us" have taken.
4. Status Quo Bias → [H101](#h101-to-h109): The tendency to maintain routines once they have been established, even when change is beneficial.

**Emotional direction:** the fourteen security feelings in [Cautious Security](#cautious-security), with more protective capability and confidence as the positive aim.

**Brand and archetype:** INNOCENT through simplicity, security and safety. The brand works towards a world where people feel safe and free from complexity or harm, gives customers tools to experience security and relaxation, lowers anxiety and promotes trust. It makes life feel beautiful, uncomplicated and, most of all, secure and safe. For more on the archetype, see *The Hero and the Outlaw*. The brand is driven to maximise protection, brings substantial experience and expertise, and takes every precaution to increase customers' ability to deal with threats. It values the inner peace, trust and peace of mind that more safeguards bring, and focuses on detailed monitoring for risk. Position it as the go-to choice for those who prioritise safety and value expertise and reliability, with a steadfast commitment to safety and security.

**Creative and copy:** highlight the confidence behind possible success and the certainty they will feel while using the solution. Emphasise why it protects, for instance that the brand has the most experience. Emphasise the process and structure used to design the product. Show the customer's independence and how the solution contributes to their preparation for success.

**Staging:** make the viewer a witness to the preparation needed to reach the goal. The model faces away from the camera. **Pace exception:** use slower, controlled gestures and tempo, with slightly open posture, straight back and chin up, not the fast animated staging used by most optimistic profiles. Expressions are cheerful and certain, above all when a threat may be imminent. Pull back to a broader view of the product and the moment security is realised, against an abstract, slightly unfocused background.

### Security experience — both approaches

Help people protect themselves and their possessions from loss or misuse. Let them see your strong experience and expertise, your tried and true solutions with concrete reasons and examples of loss avoided, and the process and structure used to design the product or service. Let them see your collaboration with others, especially people in positions of authority: signed emails with titles like SVP Operations or Director of Compliance, and endorsements by credible agencies such as federal, state and local authorities.

## Trigger terms absent from the numbered master list

Seven trigger names in the profiles have no entry in the 109-name master list, so that mapping stays empty. X-identifiers mark six of them; they are **not master-list indices**. The seventh, Anchoring, appears in both approaches of Competence and Esteem with no identifier of either kind.

### X01 Egocentric Bias

The tendency to believe that positive results come from your individual actions rather than chance or other outside influence. It appears in both approaches of Achievement, Autonomy, Competence, Empowerment and Esteem.

### X02 Self-Justification

The tendency to justify a behaviour, and deny any negative feedback about it, in order to reach a goal. It appears in both Achievement profiles.

### X03 Conformity Effect

The Belonging and Nurturance profiles, in both approaches, describe feeling more secure in an action when others "like us" have taken it in the past and had successful outcomes. Both Security profiles describe placing higher value on actions that others "like us" have taken; a successful outcome is not part of their definition.

### X04 Reciprocity

The tendency to desire to repay, in kind, what another person has provided. It appears in both Belonging profiles.

### X05 Inequality Aversion

The tendency to desire fairness and resist incidental inequalities among populations. It appears in both approaches of Belonging and Empowerment.

### X06 Temporal Discounting

The tendency to place higher value on immediate rewards than on longer-term ones. It appears in both Engagement profiles.

## Using the model carefully

Use the model to activate the customer's goal, prime the motivation, frame the approach and trigger the action with a heuristic, across copy, visuals and the customer experience.

1. **Keep goal and approach aligned.** Cautious Achievement emphasises planned progress and avoiding obstacles; Cautious Empowerment, dependable control; Cautious Esteem, retaining recognition. Their optimistic counterparts emphasise new progress, options and recognition. In Cautious Achievement a new tool can fit when its evidence supports more reliable progress, but novelty alone is not the reason to choose it. In Cautious Empowerment new options can fit when their reliability is supported, but risk-taking itself is not the promise. Cautious Belonging's goal increases acceptance and affiliation, while its approach minimises the risk of rejection.
2. **Optimistic does not always mean fast or novel.** Optimistic Security acts promptly and prefers tested second-generation innovation and proven processes, while its staging stays slower and controlled. Optimistic Belonging uses reserved gestures and a slower cadence. Optimistic Competence and Esteem pair close-up visuals with abstract, slightly unfocused backgrounds. Nurturance works deliberately in both approaches, yet Optimistic Nurturance is staged with animated gestures and a faster cadence.
3. **Loss Aversion needs a real loss.** Nurturance and Security use H063 for avoiding a loss over an equivalent gain. Test it where a real loss is at stake, or where buyer evidence shows concern about losing care. Ownership alone does not establish loss aversion. Do not invent a threat of abandonment.
4. **Peak End Rule reads more narrowly in the profiles.** H079's master description is broad. Its local description names the intense point and the end.
5. **OCEAN and archetype do different jobs.** The OCEAN pattern fits your marketing to the customer's natural style. The archetype communicates the brand's personality and values consistently.
6. **Use the profile's staging direction.** Follow its camera gaze, posture, pace, expression, background focus and casting guidance. Cautious Esteem and Cautious Nurturance pair a lowered chin and eyes with direct eye contact to camera.
7. **Evidence rules sit with specific profiles.** Cautious Achievement treats its copy ideas as starters, with product evidence for every claimed capability or result. Cautious Empowerment shows concrete evidence of how the product provides control. Cautious Security gives evidence for the protection it claims. Optimistic Esteem does not promise social outcomes the product cannot establish.
8. **Empathy and status vary by profile.** Achievement, in both approaches, tends towards lower empathy because people and circumstances are seen as barriers to overcome. Cautious Esteem also tends towards lower empathy, because every additional person who is praised lowers their own attention and self-esteem. Belonging tends towards higher empathy, as people look for similarities and the best in others. Both Esteem profiles include status sensitivity, concealed insecurity, flattery, comparison, careful service and involving customers in decisions.
