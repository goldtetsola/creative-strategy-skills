# Reading what an ad is built to prime

Use this when an analysis needs the feeling an ad is built to prime, or how it frames its benefit. It reads one ad against the definitions in [Mindstates](mindstates.md), which has nine motivations (Achievement, Autonomy, Belonging, Competence, Empowerment, Engagement, Esteem, Nurturance and Security, each defined in [the motivations table](mindstates.md#the-nine-motivations)) and two approaches (Optimistic and Cautious), and against the cue guide in [gain and loss framing](gain-and-loss-framing.md). The result describes the ad. It is not a measurement of its buyers.

## 1. Read the ad

Take the whole ad as the evidence: every configured text option, the headlines and the call to action, and everything its capture shows, says and displays. Use only that. Do not guess from the brand or the product category, and do not read the ad's name.

## 2. Find the motivation

Find the feeling the ad promises the viewer, or promises to take away, and match it to the definition of one motivation. Weigh the opening, the headline and ideas the ad repeats more than a passing phrase. A fact about the product counts through the feeling it is offered to give.

A named persuasion technique is never evidence of a motivation by itself. Social proof, being sold out, ratings, testimonials, press quotes, authority, discounts and urgency each appear under several motivations. Look for the feeling the ad promises.

Always give one main motivation. The framework asks which one best explains the ad, so "not shown" and "can't tell" are not answers. Add a second motivation only when a different one has real support in the words or pictures.

## 3. Find the approach

Read picture and words together with the [cue guide](gain-and-loss-framing.md#cues-grouped-by-what-you-look-at), weigh the cues on each side, and do not let one cue decide. Answer Optimistic when the ad frames its benefit as something gained, and Cautious when it frames it as something avoided or protected. Answer Mixed only when strong cues point both ways and neither leads, and cite at least one cue for each side. Mixed and this weighing are this repo's rule, because the source chapter gives no rule for an ad whose cues split. A Mixed ad does not group with either side when ads are compared on what they offer.

## 4. Cite and report

Quote the words, or name the moment, that each reading rests on, exactly as the capture or the configured text has them. Say what the ad is built to prime. Do not say what buyers feel or do.

## How far to trust a reading

A reading is one reader's judgement, and another reader can make a different one. A source-workspace check on 27 ads from two clients and four other brands (8 November 2026) reported that any two of three runs of one model picked the same main motivation for about eight ads in ten and the same approach for about nine in ten, and all three agreed on 78 percent of motivations and 85 percent of approaches. A model from another family agreed with their majority on about six motivations in ten and about eight approaches in ten, and the motivations that moved were mostly Empowerment, Security, Achievement and Nurturance. A reviewer who did not know who wrote which label found the labels for 8 of 10 ads supported, 2 debatable and none unsupported. The same kind of reviewer looked at the 10 ads where the two models gave different motivations: it called all 10 ambiguous, found no label unsupported, and did not favour either model: one model's set was better on 2 ads, the other's on 1, and the two were equal on 7. The check had no trusted labels, so it measures steadiness and support, not accuracy.

So keep the feeling in the working layers, name a second motivation when one has real support, and read the ad again when a decision depends on the feeling. Do not present a reading as a finding about the buyer.

**Complete when:** every ad has a main motivation and an approach, each with quoted words, and none is stated as a fact about buyers.
