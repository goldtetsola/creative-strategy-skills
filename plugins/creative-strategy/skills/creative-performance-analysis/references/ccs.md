# SYSTEM OVERVIEW

## Core Principle Shift

**V1.0:** Start with WHO (demographics/mindstate) -> Find moments
**V2.0 default:** When market–cohort fit is material, verify it first. Then start with WHEN (regret moments) when a felt moment exists -> Determine WHO experiences it; otherwise route to Non-Moment or Identity/Positioning.

**Why:** People don't buy because they fit a persona. Specific moments often drive behavior, but not every purchase has a felt moment. Do not force regret or pain-agitation onto abstract, delayed, unaware, or identity-led purchases.

## What This System Does

Transforms creative development from demographic guessing into context-routed strategic architecture. You'll know:
- **WHICH ENTRY ROUTE** fits the buyer reality (Moment-Driven, Non-Moment, or Identity/Positioning)
- **WHEN** customers experience a felt regret that drives purchase (when present)
- **WHO** experiences the selected entry and their psychology
- **WHAT** they want to gain or avoid, based on buyer evidence
- **HOW** to preserve their identity while solving the problem
- **WHY** your creative works or fails (diagnostic capability)

## How to Use This System

This playbook captures patterns we see most often--not universal laws. Treat every framework as a heuristic that earns its keep in context.

- **Hypotheses, not laws:** Framing and execution recommendations need buyer or account evidence. There is no required loss-to-gain word ratio.
- **Questions over verdicts:** Use worksheets, matrices, and gates to prompt analysis. When something "fails" a checkbox, investigate whether the framework is misaligned rather than assuming the creative is wrong.
- **Log counter-examples:** When a winning asset breaks the pattern, document the mechanism. The system evolves by absorbing those learnings, not by forcing everything back into the same mold.

The following sections contain research prompts, strategic lenses, concept exercises and brief examples. Product and offer claims require their actual support; client requirements and permissions remain with their owner records.

## Evidence Limits Behind This Approach

Loss aversion, identity protection, and prospective thought are useful lenses for forming creative hypotheses. They do not establish universal ad effects. A loss-aversion coefficient from a study does not set an optimal copy ratio or predict a conversion lift. Verify a study and its scope before citing a numerical research claim.

**Identity buffering:** where the message threatens a buyer's self-image, test whether normalization makes the message easier to accept. Do not assume shame caused a conversion loss from the ad alone, or that every purchase needs a buffer.

**Prospective vs. pain-state register:** a recalled or imagined moment differs from an immediate problem. General mind-wandering rates do not tell you how many people see an ad while thinking about a product-relevant problem. Compare the treatments in the actual buyer context before claiming that prospective copy outperforms pain-state copy.

**Confirmation vs. persuasion:** joining a familiar thought and presenting a new argument are alternative creative choices. Match the choice to buyer evidence, decision state, and test results. Customer language can help either approach. No placement or mindstate guarantees that one register wins.

# PHASE 2: STRATEGIC ANALYSIS

### Process

**A. Mass Desire Selection**

**Clarity convention:** name one primary desire so the concept has a clear job. Supporting benefits can serve it. Do not claim that mixing desires necessarily causes cognitive dissonance or a measured performance loss.

**Framing choice:** choose loss, gain or a mixed treatment from the buyer's decision, the offer and relevant evidence. Loss-aversion research does not establish a fixed lift or a default winner for ad copy. Supporting benefits may serve the primary desire.

Conditions that may justify testing loss framing:
- High-regret situations supported by buyer evidence
- Prevention-focused mindstates (avoidance motivation)
- Tangible or financial losses at stake
- Risk-averse, compliance-driven audiences

Conditions that may justify testing gain framing:
- Achievement/status-driven products
- Promotion-focused mindstates (approach motivation)
- Aspirational or luxury categories
- Low-regret, routine purchase contexts

Loss/Gain Framing Guidance:
- Choose the emphasis that fits the buyer's motivation and the offer
- Treat comparative performance as evidence only when the test supports that comparison
- Factor in competitive norms and positioning needs without treating them as proof
- Record the framing rationale when it changes the strategy; no phrase-count worksheet is required

**Evaluation Framework:**

```
MASS DESIRE ASSESSMENT (Route-Aware)

Your Route Evidence: [Moment / belief / identity and brief description]

Possible Desires This Entry Activates:

DESIRE 1: [Name | avoidance or achievement]
What They Fear or Want: [Specific loss OR gain]
How the Entry Activates This: [Connection]

Possible comparison dimensions: urgency, recurrence and breadth of demand.
Evidence and limits: [What supports the proposed desire or leaves it uncertain]
```

### Identity and reassurance

Identity-sensitive framing can explore what the buyer values, what a problem implies about them and whether reassurance helps. It is one creative approach; the examples can also work without an identity problem.

**C. Cognitive State Calibration**

```
COGNITIVE STATE CHECK

Your viewer may not be experiencing the selected entry right now. They may be thinking about the problem or identity, or encountering a new frame. This changes how you enter.

Question 1: Is your hook entering an existing simulation or
demanding the viewer start a new one?

ENTERING (prospective register):
[ ] Hook sounds like a thought they're already having
[ ] Language mirrors internal dialogue, not advertising
[ ] Opens with reflection, curiosity, or recognition
[ ] Examples: "I keep coming back to this idea that..."
            "There's a version of this that actually works..."
            "Nobody talks about the part where..."

DEMANDING (pain-state register):
[ ] Hook describes a problem being felt right now
[ ] Language mirrors acute distress
[ ] Opens with direct agitation or interruption
[ ] Examples: "Tired of [X]?"
            "Still struggling with [Y]?"
            "[X] is ruining your [Y]."

YOUR HOOK: [Quote it]
REGISTER: [Prospective / Pain-State / Hybrid]

Note: Both registers can be valid. Placement alone does not
establish the viewer's state or require a register transition.
Choose a coherent hook/body treatment from buyer evidence.

Question 2: Does the body stay in the same register as the hook?

Possible mismatch: a prospective hook followed by disconnected
product claims. If retention drops there, inspect the transition
alongside pacing, hook accuracy, and audience fit. The curve alone
does not prove why the viewer stopped or left.

[ ] Hook and body are in the same register
[ ] If register shifts, it's intentional and earned (not abrupt)

Question 3: Does the close fit the buying decision and destination?

[ ] Narrative continuation ("See what it looks like on the other side")
[ ] Direct action ("Shop now" / a truthful limited-time offer)
Rationale: [Why this close fits the buyer, argument and destination]

Either close can fit. Test the relevant outcome when the choice matters;
neither style establishes better behavioral data or higher platform ranking.
```

Choose the register from buyer evidence and the treatment's job.
Broad social, retargeting, search and high-intent placements can each use
prospective, pain-state or coherent hybrid treatments. Record the rationale.

### Emotional entry points and hook structures

**Different emotional entry points — CCS certainty/emotion map:**
- CCS CE-Q1: Relief, hope, optimism (low -> high certainty)
- CCS CE-Q2: Fear, urgency, protection (high certainty, negative)
- CCS CE-Q3: Pride, confidence, status (high certainty, positive)
- CCS CE-Q4: Anxiety, confusion, frustration (low certainty, negative)

These are creative prompts, not measured buyer states. Growthcraft uses a different valence/arousal map: its Q3 means sad/tired, not pride. In a brief or learning record, retain the framework, axes, label and plain emotion. Do not translate between the maps by number.

**Different Hook Structures:**
- Direct moment call-out: "It's 2am and you're staring at the ceiling"
- Pattern interrupt: "Stop trying to fall asleep"
- Identity qualifier: "For people who've tried everything"
- Contrarian: "Sleep advice is backwards"
- Problem acknowledgment: "Everyone says relax. Nobody says how."
- Epistemic/authority interrupt: "New research links appearance satisfaction to cortisol levels and longevity" (works when the hook is a surprising fact or reframe, not a felt moment — common in unaware-stage and preventive categories)
- Second-order reframe: "The downstream cost of 'I've given up on how I look' isn't cosmetic" (works by surfacing a consequence the buyer hasn't connected to their current behavior)
- Category violation: "Your foundation is a health product" (works by placing a familiar product in an unexpected category — forces re-evaluation)

### Video attention definitions

Use the [canonical Hook Rate definition/mapping](evidence-rules.md#hook-rate).

- **Hold rate** = ThruPlays / three-second video plays. For videos at least 15 seconds long, this compares viewers reaching 15 seconds with those reaching three seconds. Do not label a shorter video's completion as a 15-second view.
- **Six-second continuation** = six-second video plays / three-second video plays. This is an additional early measure, not a substitute for hold rate. The calculation helper retains the internal names `deep_hold_rate` and `early_hold_rate` respectively.

For Meta, request the following raw fields together for each exact ad and reporting period. The local three-second field name is a normalized helper field, not a required API column.

| Count or check | Raw source field |
|---|---|
| Three-second plays | `actions[action_type=video_view].value` |
| ThruPlays | `video_thruplay_watched_actions[action_type=video_view].value` |
| Six-second plays, when used | `video_6_sec_watched_actions[action_type=video_view].value` |
| Hook denominator and API check | `impressions` and `video_view_per_impression` |

Calculate these only for confirmed video creatives, with compatible scope and a nonzero denominator. Missing fields remain missing. Other providers' hold definitions stay separately named. A hook/hold pattern cannot reveal the viewer's cognitive state. Interpretation and retention inspection live in [the video section of inspecting ads](inspecting-ads.md#video).


## Appendix D: Common Mistakes & Fixes

**MISTAKE 1: Generic Problem Statement**

[fail] "Struggling with [category problem]?"
[pass] "It's [time]. [Specific situation]. [Exact feeling]."

**MISTAKE 2: Mixed Mass Desires**

[fail] "Save money AND look great AND feel confident"
[pass] "Stop wasting $50 on disappointing takeout" (ONE desire)

**MISTAKE 3: Identity Threatening**

[fail] "Stop being disorganized. Get your life together."
[pass] "You're great at your job. Busy people lose track. That's what tools are for."

**MISTAKE 4: Unfounded Framing Rules**

[fail] Rejecting an ad because its loss/gain phrase count misses a fixed ratio
[pass] Choosing framing from the buyer's motivation, product truth, and valid comparative evidence

**MISTAKE 5: Marketing-Speak**

[fail] "Unlock your potential with our revolutionary solution"
[pass] "Never panic at 11pm Sunday again"

# CLOSING: The Route-Aware Mandate

## What Changed in V2.0

**Hierarchy Inversion:**
- V1.0: WHO (mindstate) -> moments
- V2.0: Verify market–cohort fit when material, then route from buyer evidence: felt moment -> Moment-Driven; otherwise Non-Moment or Identity/Positioning

**Framing:**
- Choose loss, gain, or mixed emphasis from the brief and buyer evidence. Earlier numerical ratios are not operating requirements or empirically derived copy targets.

**Identity Buffering:**
- V1.0: Identity aspiration
- V2.0: Identity protection + normalization
- Use identity permission or aspiration when that is the buyer's job. Protect identity and use normalization when the message could threaten the buyer's self-image.

**Language Accessibility:**
- V1.0: Technical precision
- V2.0: Technical precision + human translation

## Intended Outcomes

The system aims to make buyer assumptions explicit, keep claims truthful, and support useful tests. Following it does not guarantee connection, conversion lift, or faster execution. Retain the parts that improve the decision and revise them when evidence challenges the explanation.
